# Recording Guide (Beginner Setup)

## Software (all free)
- **OBS Studio**: records gameplay. Settings below.
- **Roblox desktop app**: record on PC if you can. Mobile footage is harder to edit and looks worse.
- **Audacity**: record and clean voiceover.

## OBS settings (good starting point)
- Output: **1920×1080, 60 fps**
- Encoder: hardware (NVENC for Nvidia, AMF for AMD, QuickSync for Intel) if available
- Rate control: CQP / CRF, quality around **20**
- Recording format: **MKV** (it survives crashes), then remux to MP4 in OBS (File → Remux)
- Put **game audio and mic on separate tracks** so you can fix volume while editing.

## In-game settings
- Graphics quality: as high as your PC can hold at a smooth framerate
- Hide UI you don't need when doing "cinematic" shots
- Use **Shift Lock** and smooth camera moves. Avoid spinning the camera wildly.

## Mic and voice
- Any USB mic beats a headset mic. Get close (a fist away), in a room with soft stuff (bed, curtains).
- Record **live reactions** while playing (they're gold) AND do a cleaner **voiceover** afterwards for explanations.
- Energy: sound about 20% more excited than you feel. It reads as normal on video.

## While recording
- **Record everything.** Long recordings are fine, storage is cheap.
- Say "MARK" out loud when something good happens, so you can find it fast in the waveform.
- Keep a notes file open: `[time] what happened`. This becomes your footage notes for scripting.
- For each myth: get a **clean shot of the attempt and a clear shot of the result.**
- Grab **B-roll**: wide shots of the map, your character walking, the monster/NPC, the title screen. Editors always need more of this.
- For long challenges: show proof of time passing (day counter, in-game clock, a real clock on screen).

## Playing with others
- Public servers give you random reactions for free. Ask people to help test things. They usually will.
- Friends on Discord = co-stars. Record your Discord call audio on its own track.
- Kid-safety: no personal info on screen, blur usernames if someone seems uncomfortable, keep it family-friendly (it's a kids' audience and YouTube treats it that way).
