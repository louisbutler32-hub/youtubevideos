# Script Writing: How to Not Sound Like AI

Our videos are gameplay with a voiceover on top. The "script" is mostly **the voiceover you record after you've got the footage**, plus a plan of what to test before you record.

## The golden rule
**Write how you'd tell your mate what happened, out loud.** Then read it out loud. If you wouldn't say it to a friend, cut it.

## Banned phrases and habits (instant AI smell)
- "In this video…", "Today we're going to…", "Welcome back to the channel"
- "Let's dive in", "buckle up", "without further ado", "stay tuned"
- "You won't believe…" (show it instead)
- "Embark", "journey", "delve", "ultimate", "epic adventure", "game-changer", "a testament to"
- Three adjectives in a row ("a wild, crazy, insane myth")
- "Not only… but also…", "It's not just X, it's Y"
- Perfect grammar everywhere. Real speech has fragments. "Nope. Didn't work."
- Explaining the joke after the joke
- Ending every section with a neat summary sentence
- Lots of dashes and semicolons. Speech doesn't have them.

## What real narration sounds like (from the 5M video)
- "Apparently, you can trap the owl." → short, starts with the claim
- "Yo. Wait. It's true." → the reaction IS the line
- "Yeah, that was a lie." → verdict in 5 words
- "This TikToker is a liar." → opinion, personality
- "I spent a lot of time making tons of corpse piles. But this just never happened for me." → admitting failure is funny and believable

## The structure (every long video)

### 1. Cold open: first 10 seconds
- **Start mid-action on your best quick payoff.** No intro. No name. No "hey guys".
- Show 2–3 of your best quick results back to back, about 5–8 seconds each.

### 2. The promise: 10–30 seconds
One or two sentences that give:
- **The number** (100 myths)
- **The ladder** (starting with 5-minute ones, ending with one that took 10 hours)
- **The thing you have to stay for** (name the finale, make it sound insane)

Template: *"Those were just [3] of [100] [myths] I'm testing in [game]. We're starting with ones that take [5 minutes], and ending with one that took me [10 straight hours]."*

### 3. The ladder: the body
- Group content into tiers that get longer and harder.
- Quick ones: 10–20 seconds each. Claim → test → verdict. Done.
- Long ones: tell a mini story. Plan → struggle → twist → result.
- **Each tier must feel bigger than the last.**

### 4. Re-hooks: every 2–3 minutes
At the end of every tier, say how far we've come and tease two specific upcoming moments:
*"Those all took under 5 minutes. But soon we're testing ones that took hours, like whether you can [crazy thing] or [crazier thing]."*

Other re-hook types:
- **Open loop:** "And this one ended up being the craziest thing in the whole video… but first."
- **Stakes:** "If this doesn't work, I lose everything I've grinded for 3 hours."
- **Community:** "Half of you in the comments said this was fake. So let's settle it."

### 5. The finale
- The longest, hardest, most visual test.
- Show the time passing (day counters, clock overlay, "4 hours later").
- Show the pay-off clearly. Slow-mo, zoom, sound effect.

### 6. Ending: 5–10 seconds max
- No long outro. Payoff, then one line, then end screen.
- e.g. "If you know a myth I missed, comment it. I'm testing them in the next one."
- That line gets comments AND gives you the next video's research.

## Line-level tips
- **Start myths with the same word every time** (WOLF uses "Apparently…"). Pick our own catchphrase, e.g. "Supposedly…", "Rumour is…", "TikTok says…".
- **Give verdicts fast.** "It works." "Fake." "Kinda true?"
- **Use specific numbers.** "47 tries", "3 hours 12 minutes", not "a lot of tries".
- **Let footage talk.** If the clip is funny with no narration, say nothing.
- **Talk to the game.** Talking to NPCs, monsters and random players is personality for free.
- **Be honest when it fails.** Failed myths are just as watchable. "Myth busted" is a payoff.

## Before recording: the test plan
In `brief.md`, list every myth/challenge with:
- Where it came from (link)
- How you'll test it (steps)
- Rough time it'll take (→ which tier)
- Why it's interesting (is it visual? surprising? controversial?)

Then you record with purpose, and the script almost writes itself.

## Asking Claude for a script
Give Claude:
1. `brief.md` with the test list
2. Your footage notes: what happened in each test, funny moments, timestamps
3. Ask: "Write the voiceover for this using the script-writing playbook. Keep it in my voice."

Then **read it out loud and change anything that doesn't sound like you.**
